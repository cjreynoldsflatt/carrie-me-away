# 08 — Capital, Tax & Succession Plan

## Purpose

This document explains the intended member-level economics of CMA-I, including:

- Carrie's capital;
- Cameron's Service-Based Profits Interest;
- reverse vesting;
- distributions;
- sales;
- tax treatment;
- succession.

---

### Carrie

Provides approximately:

## $500,000

of initial investment capital.

### Cameron

## $0 cash

from the Grant Date.

# 6. Reverse Vesting

The full 49% is granted at the beginning, but part remains subject to forfeiture.

| Completed Service | Nonforfeitable | Forfeitable |
|---|---:|---:|
| Grant | 0.0% | 49.0% |
| Year 1 | 9.8% | 39.2% |
| Year 2 | 19.6% | 29.4% |
| Year 3 | 29.4% | 19.6% |
| Year 4 | 39.2% | 9.8% |
| Year 5 | 49.0% | 0.0% |

---

# 7. Current Economics During Reverse Vesting

While Cameron continues providing the required services, residual economics are generally:

### Carrie

## 51%

### Cameron

## 49%

from the Grant Date.

Reverse vesting affects Cameron's right to **keep** the full 49%, not his normal current share of residual distributions.

# 9. If Cameron Stops Working

If Cameron voluntarily stops providing the required services:

- the nonforfeitable portion remains Cameron's;
- the remaining forfeitable portion is lost;
- future distributions then adjust to the reduced permanent interest.

Example after Year 2:

### Cameron keeps

## 19.6%

### Cameron forfeits

## 29.4%

---

# 10. Death or Disability

If Cameron dies or becomes permanently disabled before full vesting:

## The entire remaining forfeitable portion immediately becomes nonforfeitable.

Cameron therefore holds the full:

## 49%

# 20. Intended Tax Treatment of Cameron's Interest

The parties intend Cameron's 49% interest to be treated as a genuine service-based partnership profits interest.

The intended characteristics include:

- no right to Carrie's existing capital;
- no current liquidation value attributable to Carrie's contribution;
- substantial ongoing services;
- full profits interest from the Grant Date;
- genuine reverse vesting;
- real forfeiture risk;
- treatment as a partner from the applicable grant date;
- no guaranteed return.

---

# 21. Why Reverse Vesting Matters

Reverse vesting means:

> Cameron receives the full 49% profits interest now, but must continue providing substantial services to keep all of it permanently.

It does not mean Cameron economically earns only 9.8% of current profits each year.

# 26. Gift-Tax Intent

The structure is intended so Cameron's Service-Based Profits Interest represents compensation for substantial services and not a gratuitous transfer of Carrie's existing capital.

Key facts include:

- Carrie retains her contributed-capital rights;
- Cameron has no claim on Carrie's existing $500,000;
- Cameron's interest relates to future residual economics;
- Cameron performs substantial ongoing services;
- the full 49% interest is subject to genuine five-year reverse vesting;
- the still-forfeitable portion can actually be lost if Cameron stops performing the required services;
- Cameron initially receives no salary or guaranteed annual payment.

---

# 27. Annual Documentation

Each year CMA-I should document:

- Cameron's continued substantial services;
- Cameron's current nonforfeitable percentage;
- Cameron's remaining forfeitable percentage;
- member capital records;
- distributions;
- tax allocations.

# 28. Core Economic Rules

1. **Carrie initially contributes approximately $500,000.**
2. **Cameron initially contributes $0 cash.**
3. **Carrie's existing capital remains attributable to Carrie.**
4. **Cameron receives the full 49% Service-Based Profits Interest at grant.**
5. **Residual economics are generally 51% Carrie / 49% Cameron from Day 1.**
6. **The 49% interest is subject to five-year reverse vesting.**
7. **9.8 percentage points become nonforfeitable each year.**
8. **Reverse vesting controls forfeiture, not Cameron's current distribution share.**
9. **If Cameron stops working, he keeps the nonforfeitable portion and loses the forfeitable portion.**
10. **If Cameron dies or becomes permanently disabled, all remaining forfeitable interest immediately becomes nonforfeitable.**
11. **Cameron initially receives no salary or guaranteed payment.**
12. **Future capital belongs to whoever contributes it.**
13. **Property sale proceeds return applicable capital before residual value is split.**
14. **If Carrie dies, her interest passes to Jay Flatt.**
15. **If Cameron dies, his fully accelerated 49% passes to Jay Flatt.**
