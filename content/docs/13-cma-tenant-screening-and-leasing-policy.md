## Purpose

This policy is the source of truth for objective applicant screening and selection.

DoorLoop and its screening providers may collect data and reports, but CMA-PM owns the criteria, queue, decision, notices, accommodations, and records.

---

# 1. Core Rule

CMA-PM applies the same written criteria to similarly situated applicants.

Do not decide based on:

- appearance;
- clothing or shoes;
- vehicle cleanliness;
- personality or subjective “gut feeling”;
- family composition beyond lawful occupancy;
- any protected characteristic.

Criteria may be changed prospectively for a property or market, but not invented after reviewing a particular applicant.

---

# 2. Screening Framework

## Identity

Is the applicant who they say they are?

Possible evidence:

- government-issued ID;
- screening-provider identity verification;
- consistent names, addresses, and dates across documents;
- fraud alerts or mismatches resolved before approval.

## Financial Ability

Can the applicant reasonably meet the tenant-responsible obligations?

Possible evidence:

- verified income;
- lawful rental assistance;
- credit and housing-payment history;
- recurring debt;
- move-in funds;
- guarantor where permitted.

## Rental History

Does documented rental history show reliable lease performance?

Possible evidence:

- landlord verification;
- payment history;
- unpaid landlord balances;
- material lease violations;
- eviction judgments;
- documented tenant-caused damage;
- reason for leaving.

“Behavioral” review means objective rental conduct, not personality judgment.

---

# 3. Income Standard

Default target:

## Gross household resources equal to at least 3.0× monthly rent

CMA-PM may adopt another written standard for a property or lawful program before applications open.

All lawful sources of income must be evaluated consistently.

---

# 4. Vouchers and Rental Assistance

CMA-PM will not reject or disadvantage an applicant because lawful rent is paid in whole or part through a voucher, subsidy, benefit, or other protected source of income.

For a voucher-assisted tenancy:

- count the verified subsidy amount toward the total rent obligation;
- evaluate the applicant's ability to pay the **tenant-responsible portion**, utilities, and other lawful tenant obligations;
- do not require the applicant's personal income to equal 3× the subsidy-funded portion;
- apply the same identity, rental-history, occupancy, and other lawful criteria;
- complete program inspections, contracts, and payment setup.

Example:

```text
Monthly rent                         $2,500
Verified voucher portion             $1,800
Tenant-responsible rent                $700
```

The financial review should focus on the $700 tenant portion and other lawful tenant obligations, not require the household to personally earn 3× $2,500 solely because the gross contract rent is $2,500.

Exact program rules control where they require a different calculation.

---

# 5. Income Verification

Possible documentation:

- recent pay stubs;
- employer verification;
- tax returns or business records for self-employment;
- benefit or award letters;
- verified voucher documentation;
- recurring bank deposits;
- other reasonable proof.

Avoid relying on one unusually high pay period if temporary overtime, bonus, or commission materially inflates normal income.

Applicant bank account numbers may be redacted except where reasonably necessary for verification.

---

# 6. Credit Standard

Working baseline:

## 650+ preferred

The full profile controls. Consider:

- housing-related payment history;
- serious delinquency;
- collections;
- bankruptcy timing and recovery;
- debt load;
- recent pattern;
- documented explanation.

A lower score does not automatically require denial, and a high score does not automatically require approval.

CMA-PM may revise the numeric baseline prospectively based on market results and legal review.

---

# 7. Rental History

Preferred standards:

- no material unpaid landlord balance;
- no documented pattern of serious lease violations;
- no material tenant-caused damage beyond ordinary wear;
- satisfactory landlord references where reasonably available.

Where practical, verify:

- current landlord; and
- one prior landlord.

Independently confirm the reference when reasonable through property records, management-company information, or other reliable sources.

---

# 8. Eviction Records

Distinguish among:

- filing only;
- dismissal;
- judgment;
- possession judgment;
- resolved balance;
- age and relevance;
- context and lawful explanation.

Do not treat every filing as an eviction judgment.

Do not use sealed, expunged, prohibited, or otherwise unlawfully considered information.

---

# 9. Criminal History

If criminal-history screening is used, it must be:

- narrowly tailored;
- based on legitimate property/safety concerns;
- evaluated for nature, severity, recency, and evidence of rehabilitation;
- consistent with current federal, Maryland, and local fair-housing requirements.

Avoid blanket exclusions broader than reasonably necessary.

Arrest alone is not treated as proof of conduct.

---

# 10. Occupancy

Determine maximum lawful occupancy property by property.

Before listing, record:

```text
Maximum lawful occupants: [NUMBER]
Source: [LOCAL CODE / LICENSE / HOA / OTHER]
Verified date: [DATE]
```

Consider applicable room size, bedroom, housing, and local code requirements.

Do not rely automatically on a universal two-persons-per-bedroom rule.

Do not use occupancy rules to discriminate based on familial status.

---

# 11. Pets and Assistance Animals

Ordinary pet rules are property-specific.

Service animals and qualifying assistance animals are handled under the reasonable-accommodation process, not ordinary pet screening.

Do not apply prohibited pet fees, deposits, breed/size limits, or pet rent to an approved assistance animal.

The tenant remains responsible for actual damage caused by an animal where permitted by law.

---

# 12. Move-In Funds

Before final possession, the approved applicant must provide or verify the lawful required funds, including:

- first rent payment;
- security deposit;
- other amounts expressly permitted by law and the lease.

CMA-PM should allow sufficient time for electronic funds to clear or become reliably confirmed before key delivery.

---

# 13. Applicant Documents

CMA-PM may request information reasonably related to the written criteria, including:

- photo ID;
- income documentation;
- landlord contacts;
- voucher/program documents;
- pet information for ordinary pets;
- guarantor documents;
- other lawful supporting records.

Cross-check names, addresses, dates, employer, income, and application answers.

Collect only what is reasonably necessary and secure it under Doc 22.

---

# 14. Application Queue and Completeness

CMA-PM uses:

## First complete qualified application

unless a lawful program or approved property-specific policy requires another method.

## Complete Application

An application becomes complete when CMA-PM has received:

- the submitted online application;
- all required adult applications;
- required identity information;
- required income/supporting documents;
- landlord references or enough information to request them;
- screening authorization;
- any lawful application/screening fee;
- any other item stated in the published application instructions.

The DoorLoop timestamp or other documented timestamp establishes queue order.

A missing item means the application is pending, not complete.

CMA-PM should promptly notify the applicant of missing items.

## Qualified Application

A complete application is qualified when it satisfies all written criteria after lawful verification and screening.

CMA-PM offers the property to the first complete application that becomes qualified.

If multiple applications become complete at the same recorded time, use the earliest original application-submission timestamp, and if still tied, a documented random selection.

Do not hold a qualified first applicant merely to search for someone “better.”

---

# 15. Application and Screening Fees

Before charging a fee:

- confirm current Maryland and local law;
- disclose the amount and purpose;
- identify which portion goes to a third-party screening provider;
- provide required application language;
- refund amounts above actual permitted expenses where law requires;
- avoid collecting screening charges from applicants CMA-PM will not actually screen.

Where practical, review the application for basic completeness before triggering a paid background report.

---

# 16. Conditional Approval

Permitted conditions must be adopted prospectively and applied consistently.

Examples:

- additional lawful documentation;
- qualified guarantor;
- qualified co-applicant;
- completion of voucher/program steps;
- verified resolution of a discrepancy.

Do not use an unlawful excess security deposit as a risk-adjustment tool.

Any consumer-report-based condition may require an adverse-action notice.

---

# 17. Reasonable Accommodations and Modifications

CMA-PM will maintain a written process for disability-related requests.

The process should:

- accept requests in accessible formats;
- ask only for information permitted by law;
- keep medical/disability records separate and restricted;
- respond promptly;
- engage in an interactive process;
- document the decision;
- avoid retaliation.

Consult qualified counsel for uncertain requests.

---

# 18. Maryland Fair Housing

CMA-PM must comply with applicable federal, Maryland, and local protected-class requirements, including Maryland protections relating to lawful source of income and military status.

Marketing, showings, screening, accommodations, lease terms, enforcement, renewal, and move-out must be administered consistently.

---

# 19. Decision and Adverse Action

Document each decision as:

- Approved;
- Conditionally Approved;
- Denied;
- Withdrawn / Incomplete.

Record the objective reason tied to the policy.

If a consumer report contributes in whole or part to denial, a guarantor/co-signer requirement, higher lawful cost, or another adverse term, send the required adverse-action notice with the consumer-reporting agency information and applicant rights.

Do not disclose another applicant's private information.

---

# 20. Listing Summary of Criteria

Each listing should summarize the major requirements.

```text
Rental Requirements

• Gross household resource target: 3× monthly rent, adjusted appropriately for verified rental assistance
• Credit: 650+ preferred, with the overall profile considered
• Verifiable rental history
• No material unpaid landlord balance
• Maximum lawful occupancy: [X]
• Lease term: [TERM]
• Security deposit: [AMOUNT]
• Pet policy: [POLICY]

CMA-PM uses the first complete qualified application and applies written criteria and applicable law.
```

Avoid demographic or subjective language.

---

# 21. Record Retention

Retain screening-policy versions, applications, queue/timestamps, verification, decisions, adverse-action notices, accommodation records, and related correspondence for the period set in Docs 19 and 22.

Accommodation/medical information should be stored separately with restricted access.

---

# 22. Annual Review

At least annually, review:

- income and credit standards;
- application queue;
- voucher/source-of-income treatment;
- occupancy;
- application fees;
- consumer-report vendor and forms;
- criminal-history policy;
- accommodation/assistance-animal process;
- protected classes;
- record retention;
- market results and denial patterns.

Changes apply prospectively.
