# 13 — CMA Tenant Screening & Leasing Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines how CMA-PM evaluates applicants and moves approved applicants into a lease.

DoorLoop or another approved platform may perform data collection and screening, but CMA-PM remains responsible for the decision criteria.

## 1. Objective Screening

CMA-PM should apply the same written standards to similarly situated applicants.

Do not screen based on appearance, clothing, shoes, vehicle cleanliness, personality, subjective “gut feeling,” or protected characteristics.

## 2. Screening Workflow

```text
Application
    ↓
Applicant Authorization
    ↓
Identity / Credit / Rental / Eviction Screening
    ↓
Income Review
    ↓
Prior Landlord Verification
    ↓
Apply CMA Written Criteria
    ↓
Approve / Conditional / Deny
```

## 3. Income

Target gross household income:

## [3.0] × monthly rent

unless CMA adopts another written standard.

Accept lawful income consistently.

Additional verification may include pay stubs, employer verification, tax returns for self-employment, benefit documentation, recurring income records, or other reasonable proof.

Avoid relying on one unusually high pay period if overtime or temporary income materially distorts typical compensation.

## 4. Rental History

CMA-PM may verify:
- tenancy dates;
- rent amount;
- payment history;
- unpaid landlord balances;
- material lease violations;
- damage beyond ordinary wear;
- reason for leaving;
- whether prior landlord would rent again.

When practical, independently verify the landlord reference.

## 5. Eviction Records

Distinguish:
- filing only;
- dismissal;
- judgment;
- possession judgment;
- resolved balance;
- age/relevance.

Do not automatically treat every filing as equivalent to an eviction judgment.

## 6. Credit

Adopt a written standard before reviewing applications.

CMA Credit Standard:

**[STANDARD]**

Potential considerations:
- credit score;
- housing-related payment history;
- significant delinquency;
- collections;
- bankruptcies;
- overall credit profile.

## 7. Criminal History

If used, criminal-history screening must be narrowly tailored and consistent with current federal, Maryland, and local requirements.

Avoid overly broad blanket exclusions.

## 8. Security Deposit

Do not use an unlawful excess security deposit as a risk-adjustment tool.

Follow current Maryland law and applicable exceptions.

## 9. Conditional Approval

Permitted examples may include:
- additional documentation;
- qualified guarantor;
- qualified co-applicant.

Rules should be adopted before being applied to a specific applicant.

## 10. Pets and Assistance Animals

Ordinary pet policy:

**[POLICY]**

Service animals and qualifying assistance animals must be handled separately under applicable law and should not be treated as ordinary pets.

## 11. Lease Generation

```text
Approved Applicant
      ↓
DoorLoop Maryland Lease
      ↓
CMA Required Clauses
      ↓
Property-Specific Addenda
      ↓
Tenant Review
      ↓
E-Signature
```

## 12. CMA Lease Requirements

The lease should clearly identify:

**Landlord:** applicable Property LLC

**Manager / Agent:** CMA Property Management LLC

Potential addenda:
- HOA;
- parking;
- pet rules;
- smoking;
- lawn care;
- snow removal;
- HVAC filters;
- septic;
- utilities;
- renter's insurance;
- exterior modifications;
- satellite dishes;
- property-specific requirements.

## 13. Local-Law Check

Before finalizing the screening or lease workflow for a property, complete the exact-address compliance review in CMA's Maryland compliance playbook.

County and municipal requirements may differ.
