# 1. Operating Model

```text
Property LLC
└── landlord / property owner

CMA-PM
├── manager and agent
├── tenant operations
├── rent administration
├── maintenance oversight
└── property-level records

DoorLoop
└── operational tenant platform

CMA App
└── property analysis, capital, reserves, actuals, owner reporting
```

CMA-PM manages only commonly owned Property LLCs unless a separate licensing review authorizes another arrangement.

# 2. Core Stack

- **Stable:** business address, mail scanning, registered agent
- **Google Workspace:** email, files, calendar
- **Google Voice:** business phone
- **Bluevine:** operating banking
- **Maryland-branch bank:** security-deposit account
- **DoorLoop:** listings, applications, screening, leases, rent, portal, records
- **Super:** direct-to-tenant routine maintenance when eligible
- **CMA App:** property analysis, bookkeeping/actuals, reserves, capital, economics


# 4. Listing and Leasing

CMA-PM should:

- set rent using current comparable rentals;
- market only when the property is substantially show-ready;
- use accurate reusable photos;
- state key criteria and logistics in the listing;
- use structured showing windows;
- apply the first complete qualified application rule;
- generate the final Maryland lease in DoorLoop;
- review it against Doc 10;
- archive the signed lease in DoorLoop and Google Drive.

See Docs 10, 13, and 17.

---

# 5. Rent Collection

DoorLoop is the tenant-facing rent system.

Rent settles to the CMA-PM Operating Account and is mapped to the correct Property LLC.

CMA-PM reconciles DoorLoop to banking and property books monthly.

Bluevine recurring invoices are not the default rent workflow while DoorLoop is used.

---

# 6. Security Deposits

DoorLoop may track deposit liability, but Maryland deposit funds must be held in the compliant separate account under Doc 06.

CMA-PM maintains tenant-by-tenant records and reconciles monthly.

---

# 7. Maintenance

## Routine Super-Eligible Issue

```text
Tenant contacts Super directly
      ↓
Super schedules/completes service
      ↓
Material outcome retained for CMA-PM records
```

## CMA-PM Escalation

CMA-PM directly handles:

- emergencies;
- denied/delayed Super requests;
- major or recurring repairs;
- owner-approval items;
- insurance, habitability, HOA, or compliance issues;
- unresolved tenant/vendor problems.

DoorLoop remains the material maintenance record.

CMA-PM maintains direct emergency plumber, HVAC, electrician, locksmith, and restoration contacts.

See Doc 15.

---

# 8. Communication

Routine tenant communication should use:

- DoorLoop;
- Google Voice;
- `cmapropertymanagement.com` email.

Avoid routine use of personal phone or email.

Material communication and notices should be retained.

# 9. Inspections and Property Care

CMA-PM coordinates:

- move-in inspection/photos;
- move-out inspection/photos;
- lawful scheduled interior inspections;
- exterior reviews;
- maintenance/insurance/license inspections;
- recurring filter and safety checks.

See Docs 14, 15, and 18.

# 10. Delinquency

Use a documented process:

```text
Balance becomes delinquent
      ↓
DoorLoop ledger updated
      ↓
Lawful fee / reminder
      ↓
Current Maryland/local notice
      ↓
Document delivery
      ↓
Counsel/court escalation if needed
```

Do not manage delinquency solely through informal promises, and do not create unsupported fees.

---

# 11. Renewal Rent Review

Begin approximately 120 days before a fixed-term lease expires.

```text
~120 days
→ market comps + tenant/property review

~105 days
→ renewal decision

before legal deadline
→ compliant written notice
```

Consider market rent, tenant reliability, turnover cost, vacancy risk, property expenses, and size of increase.

A strong tenant may justify remaining modestly below market when retention produces the better net result.

See Doc 17.

# 12. Bookkeeping and Reporting

CMA-PM may centralize operating banking, but property accounting remains separate.

The CMA App and books should show:

- income/expenses;
- reserve;
- deposits;
- maintenance;
- Property-Associated Capital;
- member loans;
- actual-vs-projected results.

See Doc 06.

# 13. Compliance and Risk

CMA-PM follows:

- Doc 07 for property compliance;
- Doc 19 for annual/event calendar;
- Doc 20 for insurance;
- Doc 22 for data/privacy/continuity.

Before first rent collection, the agency agreement and Maryland common-ownership collection filing must be complete.

---

# 14. Growth and Outside Management

Self-management remains the starting model.

Reevaluate when:

- the portfolio consumes too much time;
- properties are geographically dispersed;
- compliance or maintenance complexity increases;
- outside management creates better economics;
- a backup operator is needed.

If an outside manager is hired, CMA-PM still monitors rent, delinquency, maintenance, vendors, inspections, reserves, compliance, and performance.
