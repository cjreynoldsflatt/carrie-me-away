# 18 — CMA Wear, Tear & Security Deposit Guide

## Core Principle
Tenants should not be charged for normal aging and ordinary use.

Security-deposit deductions should be based on actual damage beyond ordinary wear and other lawful amounts.

## Usually Ordinary Wear
Examples:
- faded paint from age/sunlight;
- minor wall scuffs;
- small ordinary picture nail holes;
- worn carpet in traffic areas;
- minor floor wear;
- loose handles caused by age;
- faded blinds;
- minor countertop wear;
- normal appliance aging.

## Usually Potential Damage
Examples:
- large holes in drywall;
- excessive nail/anchor holes;
- broken doors;
- broken windows caused by misuse;
- burns;
- large stains;
- pet urine damage;
- chewed trim;
- missing fixtures;
- broken cabinets;
- unauthorized paint/alterations;
- TV-mount or large shelving holes;
- damage worsened by failure to report a problem.

## Picture Nail Holes
CMA working policy:

**Small ordinary picture nail holes are generally treated as normal wear.**

Potential chargeable damage may include:
- excessive holes;
- large anchors;
- lag bolts;
- TV-mount holes;
- substantial patching;
- drilling into tile, cabinetry, masonry, or sensitive surfaces.

Evaluate the actual repair required, not merely the existence of a hole.

## Age Matters
Consider:
- age;
- expected useful life;
- preexisting condition;
- ordinary depreciation;
- actual restoration cost.

Do not automatically charge the full replacement cost of an old item.

## Move-In Documentation
At move-in:
- take comprehensive date-stamped photos;
- photograph each room;
- photograph existing damage;
- record appliance condition;
- record floors/walls;
- allow tenant to report preexisting issues.

Recommended:
```text
Wide photo
+
Close-up
+
Written note
```

## Move-Out Documentation
At move-out:
- repeat the same photo sequence;
- compare with move-in;
- identify ordinary wear separately;
- photograph each claimed damage item;
- obtain estimate/invoice when appropriate.

## Deduction File
For each deduction retain:
- move-in photo;
- move-out photo;
- description;
- estimate/invoice if appropriate;
- amount charged;
- basis under lease/law.

## Do Not Charge for Routine Turnover
Routine turnover may include:
- standard cleaning between tenants;
- repainting due to normal aging;
- normal carpet wear;
- minor scuffs;
- ordinary maintenance.

These should not automatically become tenant deductions.

## Security Deposit Workflow
```text
Tenant Moves Out
      ↓
Move-Out Photos
      ↓
Compare to Move-In
      ↓
Separate Wear from Damage
      ↓
Document Lawful Charges
      ↓
Prepare Itemization
      ↓
Return Remaining Deposit
```

Follow current Maryland timing, notice, interest, inspection, and itemization requirements.
