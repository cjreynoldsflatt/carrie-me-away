## Purpose

This document is the source of truth for CMA-PM's ordinary-wear policy, move-in and move-out evidence, and Maryland security-deposit closeout process.

## 1. Core Principle

Tenants should not be charged for normal aging and ordinary use.

A security deposit is not liquidated damages and should not be forfeited merely because a lease was broken. Deductions must reflect lawful, actual damages or other amounts permitted by law and the lease.

## 2. Maryland Deposit Controls

Before collecting or administering a deposit, confirm current law. Under the current Maryland framework:

- the ordinary cash security-deposit cap is generally one month's rent, subject to a narrow statutory utility-assistance exception;
- the receipt required by law must be included in the written lease;
- the funds must be placed within 30 days in an interest-bearing account devoted exclusively to security deposits at a Maryland branch of a federally insured institution;
- the aggregate deposit-bank balance must cover all deposits for which the landlord is liable;
- the deposit and required interest generally must be returned within 45 days after the tenancy ends, less lawful deductions.

Use the Maryland interest rate or official calculator applicable to the tenancy.

## 3. Required Inspection-Rights Notice

The security-deposit receipt must advise the tenant of applicable rights, including:

- a move-in inspection in the tenant's presence if requested by certified mail within 15 days after occupancy;
- a move-out inspection in the tenant's presence if the tenant gives the required certified-mail notice at least 15 days before the intended move;
- the landlord's obligation to schedule the move-out inspection within five days before or five days after the stated move date and provide the required notice.

CMA-PM should preserve the request, notices, inspection report, and proof of mailing.

Failure to provide required rights or follow required inspection procedures can impair the right to withhold for damage.

## 4. Usually Ordinary Wear

Examples:

- faded paint from age or sunlight;
- minor wall scuffs;
- small ordinary picture nail holes;
- worn carpet in traffic areas;
- minor floor wear;
- age-related loose hardware;
- faded blinds;
- minor countertop wear;
- normal appliance aging.

## 5. Usually Potential Damage

Examples:

- large drywall holes;
- excessive nail or anchor holes;
- broken doors or windows caused by misuse;
- burns;
- large stains;
- pet urine or chewing damage;
- missing fixtures;
- broken cabinets;
- unauthorized paint or alterations;
- TV-mount or heavy-shelving holes;
- damage worsened by failure to report a problem.

The facts, age of the material, preexisting condition, and actual repair required control.

## 6. Picture Nail Holes

CMA-PM's working policy:

> Small ordinary picture nail holes are generally treated as ordinary wear.

Potential chargeable damage may include:

- excessive holes;
- large anchors or lag bolts;
- TV-mount holes;
- substantial patching;
- drilling into tile, cabinetry, masonry, exterior surfaces, or other sensitive material.

## 7. Useful Life and Betterment

Do not charge a tenant as though an old item were new.

Consider:

- age;
- expected useful life;
- preexisting condition;
- ordinary depreciation;
- actual restoration cost;
- whether the replacement improves the property beyond its prior condition.

## 8. Move-In Evidence

Before or at move-in:

- take date-stamped photos of every room and material surface;
- take wide and close-up photos of existing defects;
- document appliances, fixtures, keys, and access devices;
- provide the tenant the required opportunity to identify existing conditions;
- preserve the tenant's written comments.

Recommended evidence:

```text
Wide photo
+
Close-up
+
Written note
```

## 9. Move-Out Evidence

At move-out:

- follow the tenant's inspection rights;
- repeat the move-in photo sequence;
- compare images side by side;
- separate wear from damage;
- photograph each claimed damage item;
- obtain invoices or reasonable estimates where appropriate;
- preserve communications and forwarding-address information.

## 10. Deduction File

For each deduction retain:

- move-in evidence;
- move-out evidence;
- factual description;
- lease or legal basis;
- estimate or invoice;
- amount withheld;
- final invoice and refund of any excess if an estimate was used.

Current Maryland law permits supporting documentation through estimates in some circumstances, but when actual repair cost is lower, required notice, final invoice, and refund of excess withholding must be handled within the applicable deadline.

## 11. Ordinary Turnover Is Not Automatically Tenant Damage

Do not automatically charge for:

- standard between-tenant cleaning;
- repainting caused by ordinary aging;
- normal carpet wear;
- minor scuffs;
- ordinary preventive maintenance;
- owner-selected upgrades.

## 12. Closeout Workflow

```text
Tenancy Ends
      ↓
Move-Out Inspection / Photos
      ↓
Compare to Move-In Evidence
      ↓
Separate Wear from Damage
      ↓
Document Lawful Charges
      ↓
Calculate Required Interest
      ↓
Prepare Itemized Statement + Support
      ↓
Mail Statement and Refund Within Deadline
```

If any part is withheld, send the required itemized list and supporting documentation by the required method to the tenant's last known address within the applicable 45-day period.

## 13. Sale or Manager Transition

If the property or management relationship changes, preserve and transfer:

- original deposit amount and date;
- applicable interest-rate records;
- tenant name and last known address;
- account and ledger history;
- any deductions or pending disputes.

The Property LLC remains responsible as landlord to the extent provided by law; CMA-PM's administration does not erase the landlord's statutory obligations.
